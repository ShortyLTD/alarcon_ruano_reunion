## Executive assessment (Ploy)

The site has a strong foundation. It looks distinctive and trustworthy, performs exceptionally well, exposes real sample work, and handles factual caveats more carefully than most early products. The main conversion risk is not design quality or speed. It is product-state clarity: visitors must piece together what the short interview produces, why the current download is free, what the future $39 purchase changes, and which action to take inside the example planner.

The technical SEO baseline is also healthy — valid 200 responses, consistent canonicals, a usable sitemap, permissive robots rules, intentional noindex on /plan, and parseable structured data — but no pages appeared in a Google `site:` result during the audit. That is a discovery problem, not evidence of a crawl-blocking defect.

## Measurements reported by Ploy

- Lighthouse performance: 100 on /, /our-reunion, /guides, /plan. LCP ≈ 0.6–0.7 s.
- Lighthouse accessibility: 96 (/), 96 (/our-reunion), 95 (/guides), 93 (/plan). Repeated failures: insufficient colour contrast on all four pages; visible-label/accessible-name mismatches on /, /our-reunion and /plan; an unnamed ARIA progressbar on /plan.
- Image-delivery savings estimated at roughly 128 KiB (home) and 117 KiB (planner); unused JavaScript 85–95 KiB. Neither judged a priority given the performance scores.
- HTTP → HTTPS redirect; trailing-slash variants 308 to canonical; a nonexistent path returns a real 404; robots.txt names the sitemap; sitemap lists seven indexable URLs; /plan is `noindex, follow` and excluded from the sitemap.
- Search demand (US, DataForSEO, directional): "family reunion planner" ≈ 1,300/month; "family reunion planning checklist" ≈ 260/month; "family reunion planning kit" ≈ 10/month. Local "Santa Cruz family reunion" results were dominated by venue and provider pages; the original reunion site appeared but did not link to the kit.
- `site:santa-cruz-reunion-kit.vercel.app` returned zero organic results.

## Five highest-priority findings (Ploy)

1. **Make the hero explain the mechanism and exact output** (/). Observed conversion defect; impact a hypothesis until measured. The headline communicates category and emotional benefit but not the mechanism or the most valuable outputs; deliverables are distributed farther down; "Build my reunion preview" asks a cold visitor to interpret "build" and "preview". Fix: keep the headline, replace the supporting paragraph with one sentence naming input and output, use one primary CTA ("Start my free reunion plan"), rename the secondary CTA ("See a complete sample"), add a scannable proof row. Verify with a five-second comprehension test (5–8 people; pass if ≥ 80 % can name the interview, two outputs, and that they contact providers themselves) and, once analytics exist, primary-CTA click-through before/after.
2. **Resolve the free-preview versus future-$39 ambiguity** (/, /plan). Observed pricing ambiguity. /plan offers "Download kit" from the example plan with no nearby explanation of what is free now versus what $39 buys later; on mobile the download is the most prominent header action before personalization. Fix: one product-state statement everywhere ("The complete preview is free while checkout is being prepared. The planned launch price is $39 once per reunion."), a persistent "Preview edition · Free today" label in /plan, and — only once decided — one sentence on whether plans created now retain access after checkout launches. Do not imply hosted guest sites, shared accounts, or RSVP tracking. Verify by reviewing every occurrence of "free", "preview", "download", "complete kit", "$39", and asking five people what is free today and what would cost $39.
3. **Make personalization — not download — the dominant planner action, especially on mobile** (/plan, /plan?start=1). Observed hierarchy and mobile-friction defect; abandonment impact a hypothesis. Competing actions (Download kit, Make this plan yours, Personalize it, Explore your options, a second download); at 390×844 the interview's forward button is below the initial viewport; the progressbar lacks an accessible name. Fix: primary header action "Start my plan"/"Personalize this plan", sample download secondary and labelled as a sample, same wording for banner/hero/trigger, sticky dialog footer with Back and Continue, visible "Step 1 of 3", `aria-label` on the progressbar, preserve entered values when moving back or reopening. Verify at 320×568, 375×667, 390×844 and 200 % zoom; keyboard-only and screen-reader completion; focus trap, Escape/close, focus return.
4. **Establish actual search discovery and strengthen the path from search content to product proof** (all indexable pages). Observed discovery signal plus unconfirmed causes. Crawl configuration is sound; guide titles are about 81, 92 and 94 characters; guide landings offer little compact proof before the CTA. Fix: verify in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools, submit /sitemap.xml, request indexing; add a truthful editorial link from the original reunion site; move to an owned brand domain with one-to-one 308 redirects if the venture persists; shorten article titles; add a compact proof module to every guide article; add contextual links from /our-reunion to the guides. Verify via Search Console indexing status over time, crawlable link on the original site, rendered title lengths, query impressions after 4–8 weeks. Unconfirmed hypotheses: newness, not yet submitted, not yet linked, not yet processed.
5. **Fix the repeatable accessibility failures before wider acquisition** (/, /our-reunion, /guides, /plan). Observed defects from Lighthouse and live HTML: contrast, name mismatches, unnamed progressbar. Fix: WCAG AA (4.5:1 normal text, 3:1 large text/UI boundaries), prioritise captions, source/check dates, helper text, footer links, pale-green panels; accessible names must contain the visible label; name the progressbar; connect errors/help text with `aria-describedby`; verify focus indicators. Verify with Lighthouse/axe (zero contrast, name-mismatch, progressbar failures), keyboard-only navigation, 200 % zoom, High Contrast Mode, voice control.

## Secondary observations (Ploy)

Keep as-is: performance; technical SEO coherence; research caveats; founder chronology on /our-reunion; /your-data clarity; the coastal editorial visual system.

Lower priority: reword "First, we brought our own family together" (can read as a company accomplishment); shorten the homepage and /our-reunion titles (66 and 71 characters); add Article/Breadcrumb data to the guide hub only if accurate; image optimisation and unused-JavaScript reduction when convenient.

Recommended order: hero and CTA → product-state statement → /plan actions and sticky footer → accessibility → indexing surfaces, original-site backlink, titles, guide proof modules.

## Exact copy changes (Ploy) — summary of the replacement map

Copy rules: one primary CTA on marketing pages ("Start my free plan"); one secondary sample CTA ("See a complete sample"); call the product a free working preview or preview edition, not a free trial; call example downloads a sample kit; say provider inquiries or quote requests, not "outreach"; preserve the evidence boundaries (researched not vetted; organizer sends and confirms; guest guide is downloadable; no hosted guest sites or RSVP tracking).

Provided replacements for: hero paragraph, CTAs, reassurance line and price note; proof strip; founder section eyebrow, headline, body and caveat; six feature headings; sample section eyebrow, headline, intro, download CTA, state label and footer; how-it-works steps; pricing headline, intro, card label, benefit list, CTA, availability note and product-boundary note (with three options for the free-preview transition sentence — A: existing plans keep access; B: future downloads require purchase; C: undecided, say nothing); guide cards; eight FAQ answers; final CTA; /our-reunion hero, "What this example can — and cannot — show" card and closing section; /guides hero and product bridge; a guide-article product module; /plan header, planner hero, example banner, tab labels ("Plan", "Places", "Inquiries", "Budget & bookings", "Guest guide"), download card, interview labels, helper text and navigation buttons ("Back"/"Continue"); and metadata titles/descriptions for every page.

QA checklist supplied by Ploy: consistent CTA wording; "preview edition" inside /plan; "$39" always planned, one time, checkout not connected; no sentence implying the kit organized the June 2026 reunion; no "vetted" or "endorsed"; no hosted guest sites, shared accounts or automatic RSVP tracking; no invented hours, savings, testimonials, conversion results, confirmed bookings or exclusive access; titles render once without duplicated brand suffixes; CTA labels match visible text, accessible names and analytics event names.
