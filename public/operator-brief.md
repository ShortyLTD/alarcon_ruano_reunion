Domain purchase and renewal, hosting, payment processing, future integrations, and operating costs are separate. The original family domain and historical guest website are excluded. Private family information, personal accounts, provider contracts, exclusive rates, and third-party ownership rights do not transfer.

## Your first 30 days

**Days 1–3 — take control.** Deploy under your accounts, set a monitored inbox and domain, verify the complete organizer journey, and choose one customer segment you can already reach.

**Days 4–7 — verify the research.** Recheck the most relevant providers for your audience. Prioritize capacity by room/layout, date and price context, minimums, accessibility, parking, weather alternatives, deposits, and cancellation terms. Record facts with a source and date; label unresolved questions clearly.

**Days 8–14 — observe five organizers.** Watch them start without coaching, choose a plausible gathering place, prepare an inquiry, and share guest information. Record what helped their next decision and where they needed assistance. A download is not a sale.

**Days 15–21 — test one offer.** Pick a self-service or assisted service. Define the exact deliverable, support scope, price, and delivery process. Test payment, receipt, customer access/delivery, and refund handling before charging. A $39 consumer kit is a historical price hypothesis, not established willingness to pay.

**Days 22–30 — measure the work.** Use one relevant audience or referral channel. Track qualified conversations, useful plans, actual purchases, delivery labor, support needs, and refunds. Improve the research and inquiry templates using real provider replies.
